BM25 ranking function
BM25 is a ranking function used by search engines to estimate how relevant a document is to a query. It is based on the probabilistic retrieval framework developed in the 1970s and 1980s. For every query term, BM25 combines the inverse document frequency of the term with a saturating function of how many times the term appears in the document. Two parameters control its behaviour: k1 sets how quickly repeated occurrences stop adding to the score, and b sets how strongly the score is normalized by document length. Typical values are k1 = 1.2 and b = 0.75. BM25 remains a strong baseline and is the default similarity in many open-source search engines.
